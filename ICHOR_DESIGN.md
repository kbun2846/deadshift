# Ichor — local weapon preview revision 33

The current brief is a straight dark single-edged sword with an angled tip, a dark/red grip, fast intentional two-handed slashes, heavy stylized blood and a twelve-hit Frenzy. No thrusts. Frenzy never heals. Robots stay bloodless with body-coloured metal and yellow/blue sparks; human bodies use directional blood, flesh slices and stylized organs. Match Syphon remains a separate rule.

## Current gameplay

Slashes and Frenzy break destructible props through authored knee-high bases. The low permanent headstone stump no longer shields the breakable slate above it; full walls and normal cover still block the cut. E uses the same low-cover height rule.

- Hold LMB/Space or tap for the same flowing sequence of varied cuts: 0.24-second cadence, contact after 0.067 seconds, 30 damage at zero blood scaling linearly to 85 at 100. Range 2.15 m; 2.9-radian broad cuts, 1.85/2.3-radian short cuts and occasional full 360-degree spins. No ammo, reload or aim-in.
- A cut during a dash or within 0.24 seconds afterward uses a distinct low crossing slash: 15% more damage and 0.3 m more reach. Two dashes recharge 12% slower than default.
- Blood gains vary per successful victim: 6.5–8.5 per base/Frenzy cut (mean 7.5, formerly 9), 11.5–14.5 per E hit (mean 13, formerly 16), including robots/targets. Rapid successful attacks within 0.85 seconds add 7%, 14%, then at most 21% extra blood gain. Multiple victims in one cut do not advance the chain again; misses add nothing. Base gain remains about 17–19% lower than revision 29. Seven seconds without a hit before decay at 3/sec. Hits at full blood overflow the meter visually.
- Above 90% blood: regenerate 6 HP/sec up to maximum health. Exactly 90% does not qualify. This pauses throughout Frenzy, including the activation tick, and never revives a dead player.
- Full blood passives: 5% faster walking and E/X cooldowns refill at 1.2x. Both ability circles pulse red while this applies. Base attack/dash recharge do not get this bonus.
- Full-blood human hits cause eight seconds of cosmetic dripping, never DOT. Fresh blood trails give the Ichor wielder 20% speed and red feet; this is always a passive, including the residue from E. Trails last 18 seconds, capped at 160 samples per caster, reset with the map/loadout. Trail and full-blood boosts multiply.
- E: a literal growing blood arc, 60–80 damage, 19 m/s, 17 m reach, 6-second cooldown. Requires at least 50% blood without spending it. Below halfway it cannot cast or begin a cooldown; bots obey the same rule. The existing full-blood 1.2x recharge makes the cooldown five seconds at full. It leaves denser liquid spray, layered wakes and blood stains along its path. One hit per victim; solid cover/ground stop it. Meter rises only on actual contacts.
- X: twelve automatic authored cuts/sweeps/spins over 2.4 seconds (0.20-second intervals; still quicker than basic cuts). Starting blood determines 180–540 total direct damage if every hit lands; additional victims in an individual strike take 22% collateral. Three combo sequences, all slashes. 50-second cooldown from activation.
- Attacking applies a 12% walking slowdown; dodges are unaffected.
- WASD steers Frenzy, with movement pulses from 1.3x to 1.6x normal walking during its cuts. It never moves the player without movement input; collisions and normal terrain rules remain.
- Frenzy drains 33.333 health/sec for an 80-health full sequence, never heals or kills the wielder, stopping at one health. Practice invulnerability skips the cost. Death stops the sequence. Later contacts may fill blood, but do not change that combo's captured damage scale.

Cadence, blood gain/decay, dash bonus, trail bonus, contact geometry and Frenzy movement/cost are playtest tuning. Existing damage types ichorSlash/ichorWave/ichorFrenzy carry through networking and lethal hits, preserving weapon drops and cleanup.

## Animation, effects and HUD

The slightly longer straight blade has a dark face, angled tip, narrow cutting edge, groove details, fittings, corded red/black grip and moving red ties. One edge is sharp: the wrist rolls the blade for reverse cuts. Irregular deep-crimson blood streaks grow across both blade faces as blood builds. The coating sits one millimetre off both steel faces, in blade coordinates with no translating or scaling shell; it stays attached through flips and the held guard. Narrow liquid streaks shed from swings rather than broad square flakes. Both hands stay on fixed grip points, with finite upper/forearm lengths for local players, remote humans and robots.

Ten authored forms include high diagonals, waist cuts, compact backhands, rising cuts with rolling recovery, low dash cuts and full-body spins. Each has a short preparation, fast crossing and longer follow-through. The blade rests on the previous ending side; the next cut is chosen to flow from that guard. Moving cuts lower/lean the stance; dash cuts cross low; Frenzy includes full-body spins and extra wake echoes. Movement input blends into the pose. Slash geometry covers more blade length. Ordinary cuts blend ivory, faint red and dull red below 60% blood, then become red. Frenzy deliberately mixes separate sharp white and red crescents: crossing cuts, rising fans, broken spirals and a finishing wheel. The avatar banks, dips and turns with the cut, with both hands remaining on the grip.

Slash ribbons sample the actual ground at both edges and rise above slopes, retaining depth testing so walls and roofs still occlude them; under-bridge effects stay below. Sampling two edge points and reusing them for triangles replaces six repeated evaluations per segment.

Bloody cuts and E use deep blood-red shades with dark veins rather than bright pink-red. Swing travel sheds directional droplets even on misses, without emitting extra while paused; dry swings shed none. Human contacts produce substantially larger sprays, while robot contacts keep their metal and sparks. E has a broad crimson crescent, five thinner wakes and denser liquid spray. Droplets land on floors or nearby surfaces and leave bounded stains through the existing persistent BloodDrops pool. Airborne caps are 128/256/384/512/768 for Potato/Performance/Balanced/Quality/Extreme; droplets keep their original bridge level and use the existing line batch. Robot sparks remain yellow and blue even on Potato. Humans use heavier directional spray, clothing/wall/prop staining, liquid pool/footstep sounds and low-poly organs on death. At high blood the wielder progressively becomes drenched, nearly fully red at maximum. This recolours private copies of the existing avatar vertices in eight stages, without extra draws or shared-material mutation. Arms, hat and clothing are included; robots never use it. Full blood refreshes 24 wet steps rather than eight, with small drips beside local soaked footprints. Stains linger as blood dries; respawning cleans the living avatar, while fallen bodies retain an owned colour snapshot until cleanup. The shing is quieter, with separate fleshy/metal contacts. All audio is synthesized.

The blood meter sits below the two dash pips and above “ichor”. It has a transparent backing, ragged outline, red liquid, increasing slosh/shake/ooze and a smoothly rising percentage. Fill follows the actual amount with a frame-rate-independent easing motion (about 0.3–0.4 seconds for most of a gain); repeated hits redirect it continuously. The empty outline and lettering are subdued, while the liquid stays bright blood red with no backing. Further hits at full blood bulge it and throw small fading drops. SVG updates are capped at 30 Hz; overflow drops cap at 36. The obsolete “hits fill blood” text is gone. A halfway notch marks the E requirement. Below 50% its circle is subdued and reads 50%; the accessible label explains the requirement. E/X stay entirely on-screen, side by side on touch layouts. Health remains at the top as before.

## Held guard and timed bullet deflection

Hold RMB / Left Shift (GUARD on touch) to cross the sword across the chest. A filled white spark on the blade shows the active defence. A standard circle shows RMB / LEFT SHIFT and the cooldown, without a separate ability name. Each fresh charge rolls 28–32 damage capacity (`ICHOR.guardCapacity` 30 ± `guardRoll` 2; raised from 14–18 on 2026-10-01, about five close-range Nominal bullets); lowering and re-raising preserves what remains. It can stay raised indefinitely while moving 10% slower. Front-facing physical projectiles spend that capacity, including Omen and individual Ballast pellets. A shot exceeding the remaining capacity deals only its leftover damage. Electricity, explosions, active curses and melee bypass it. Only exhausting the capacity starts a fixed 20-second cooldown, unaffected by blood recharge; a fresh hold is needed afterwards. Lowering the blade or using E/X is free. Guarding suppresses basic cuts and can interrupt Frenzy without refunding X; E/X lower it. Server collision proxies share the real capacity, so simultaneous attackers cannot get separate budgets. The guard has a short raise, catch recoil, blade-positioned contact star, gold sparks and a steel clang. Ricochets are cosmetic and do not deal reflected damage.

The earlier timed-cut parry remains separate: a regular bullet arriving from the front during the 0.040–0.095-second blade-crossing window is knocked sideways. Only one per cut, and a 0.28-second recovery prevents Frenzy or simultaneous shooters from blocking every round. The front check is roughly a 112-degree cone around the cut's locked direction. Nominal, Sidekick and Sightline ordinary bullets qualify; Ballast pellets, Omen, electricity and explosives do not. The original round is consumed with no damage, blood or hit credit. The visible spent ricochet travels outward in a randomized direction, clips to cover/terrain and deals no reflected damage. A short metallic ping and yellow-white flecks accompany it. Host/bot collision proxies share the defender's actual guard recovery; clients cannot authorize deflections. The event is shared to other screens.

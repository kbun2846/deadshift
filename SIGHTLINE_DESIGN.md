# Sightline — implemented design

Updated 2026-09-27, preview revision 22. The owner approved implementation, the amber loaded-round cue, a long rifle slung diagonally on the back, and a holstered Sidekick. This supersedes the discussion draft. All work remains uncommitted in the isolated `codex/omen` checkout alongside Omen and the shared UI changes.

## Loadout and controls

Player-facing weapon names are Sightline and Sidekick, including the ammo rows, status, accessibility labels, controls and tutorial.

- Standing: compact Sidekick held, long rifle diagonally across the back. A leather holster holds the Sidekick when the rifle is in hand. Both appear in the shared studio weapon image.
- E toggles crouched rifle stance without cooldown. Only E enters crouch. Walking/dodging stops; the 1.33-second setup blocks rifle shots (1.5 times faster than revision 17). The rifle is drawn in 0.28 seconds, then the player settles into crouch and deploys the bipod. E can cancel setup immediately. Standing returns to Sidekick in 0.42 seconds. Crouching alone retains the ordinary camera.
- Manual Sidekick: 10 rounds, 25 ± 2 damage (integer 23–27, rolled once per shot), 0.28-second click cap, 22 m range, 2-second reload. Nominal-style red bloom guide, with stronger spread toward maximum range.
- The loadout has two dashes, shared by Sightline and Sidekick. Recharge takes 1.52 seconds per charge (5% shorter than the usual 1.6 seconds), after the unchanged 0.6-second delay. Crouch still prevents dodging.
- R or an empty-trigger reload affects the current weapon. Ordinary rifle reload takes 4.2 seconds and requires crouch. Standing cancels an unfinished ordinary rifle reload. X is the only standing rifle reload. If Breach is ready, X interrupts a normal Sightline reload and begins a fresh full 4.2-second Breach load; it cannot restart an ongoing Breach load. R immediately drops scope; aiming is blocked throughout either reload, then requires release and a fresh aim press.
- RMB / Left Shift / AIM: standing Sidekick retains its bloom cone, with no red laser or zoom. Only crouched Sightline has the public laser. Outdoors, scope gives a 45-degree forward view at 1.46× camera height, about 10% farther than revision 17. Forward lead remains 76% of rear screen reach, so aiming down places the player near the top. Positive elevation adds 0.6% reach per metre, capped at 6%. Only the view cone/camera turns with weight (0.35 rad/s, 1.1 rad/s² acceleration); laser/shot aim has slight cursor weight (rate 28, 3400 px/s cap), lighter than ordinary focused aim; Sidekick retains the previous responsive cursor. Own laser remains visible while the cone catches up, without revealing hidden enemies or their effects. Interiors and standing/burning crop tiles disable scope zoom and the laser. Holding aim there keeps the unscoped bloom and cursor-limited shot spread.

## Rifle and scope

- One round, integer damage roll 493–505. A 500 HP target can survive a low roll with 1–7 HP.
- Triggering locks the direction and blanks the laser for 0.16 seconds, then launches a physical 114 m/s round (5% slower than revision 19) with a fading wind wake. Victims can cross the path before impact.
- Rounds retain damage through every breakable prop. The sniper has an elevated, straight flight from its firing height toward cursor elevation. Low rocks/debris can be cleared when below the shot; tall cover, rising ground, deck surfaces intersecting that trajectory, and the first player stop it. The public laser uses the same flight/height tests and ends at the cursor or the first true obstruction, skipping pierced props. It does not drape or bend across terrain/bridges. A high shot crosses above the bridge; a shot from below stays below. Sidekick retains ordinary lower bullet collision.
- Aimed range is the maximum scoped view in the firing direction, adjusted for screen aspect, plus 10%. It works even without previously zooming; camera motion cannot extend a fired round. Hip shots land around cursor distance with wide ±0.26-radian spread.
- Cursor rays use the sniper flight height (1.28 m), and point aiming uses the Sightline muzzle offset (0.40 m lateral, 1.73 m forward); Sidekick retains its separate offset. The held barrel has no sideways yaw. Laser visuals attach to the animated muzzle and run continuously to the collision-tested endpoint, including during the quick draw. Only a loaded Sightline shows its public laser. Scoped cursor motion has a little weight, independent of the much slower camera.
- Red bloom brackets/fill share one firing plane and the correct Sightline/Sidekick muzzle offset. They do not take separate ground heights per corner, avoiding skew at bridge edges.
- Rifle report and directional sound HUD use 1.5× ordinary hearing distance.
- Outside the cone, scenery is gray. Remote players are concealed by the view rule; projectile/effect batches, practice targets, birds and crows use fragment concealment. Hidden actors continue simulating. Water/fog keep animating. Gray shading uses prewarmed world-space shader uniforms, not a CSS backdrop filter or terrain-projected mask. Map changes do not stack shader hooks. The extra vertical camera height is removed from fog depth, keeping distant scenery readable; no extra render pass or per-frame DOM mask is used.

## X — breach round

- First X loads for the full 4.2 seconds. Standing X holsters the Sidekick, draws the rifle for a moving reload and never crouches automatically. Finishing returns to the Sidekick with the special round in the slung rifle; E must equip it before firing. Crouched loading stays crouched.
- E can change stance during X loading without canceling it. Completed rounds survive stance changes. R cannot replace a loaded special round. Death/reset clears armed/loading cues; already launched shots keep flying.
- Amber shell band, bright yellow opening vents, raised locking plates and pale vapor identify the armed rifle, held or slung. Yellow light at the muzzle and chamber pulses with a slight flicker while five small sparks spiral from the muzzle until the round is fired. Regular loading cycles the bolt and inserts a brass shell. X uses a larger amber-banded shell and opens the vents. Sidekick has a magazine-and-hand reload. Drawing/holstering, crouch/stand, recoil and the hands follow continuous poses; the rifle is pulled clear of the back, turned outside the shoulder, then shouldered. Return follows that path backward. Sling position and reload tilt clear torso and hat; support hands transition continuously in front of the chest. Arms use fixed 0.33/0.35 m limb lengths with a compact receiver grip; unreachable transition targets cannot stretch them. The bipod folds along the rifle when stowed and opens during crouch, remaining folded during a standing Breach reload.
- From crouch, LMB or second X fires. The 50-second cooldown starts when the shot is committed. Another round cannot be reserved while armed or cooling down.
- Breach detonates at cursor distance, including hills and bridges, unless it hits a body or solid cover sooner. Ordinary aimed rounds keep their extended travel.
- Direct: 493–505 plus 300 blast, combined once as 793–805 damage. Splash: 300 at center, 250 at 1.3 m, falling to 40 at 4.5 m. Cover/terrain protect victims. The shooter is immune to both Breach blasts. The small muzzle flash/smoke/sparks are at the barrel; a separate short-range blast deals up to 80 damage, falling to zero at 1.5 m, to nearby opponents through normal cover checks. The large landing blast uses the game's grenade explosion. A very close direct victim can take muzzle damage in addition to the landing hit.
- X dial/touch button show loading, armed and cooldown. Both magazines stay visible. The active weapon has a large upper row; the stowed one has a smaller lower row. The same rows slide/scale between positions over 0.42 seconds, preserving ammo and reload fill. Rifle ammo is one large horizontal sniper-round silhouette: a flat UI shape with no realistic texture, filling left to right on reload and amber when armed.

## Integration

- Seven lessons using the current tutorial presentation, keyboard/touch controls, shared HUD and weapon cards.
- Protocol 13 carries stance/X inputs, authoritative loadout, remote poses and physical rounds. Prediction respects crouch/setup and reload aim exit only for Sightline; practice overrides remain offline.
- Bots use the same inputs, 1.33 s setup, ammo and reloads. They travel with Sidekick, plant to scan outdoor lanes for 7–10 seconds, slowly sweep with brief pauses, and relocate after an empty lane. Scoped sensing uses the same 45-degree view and directional reach, with no close rear vision. They react, settle aim, lead by the 0.16 s commitment plus flight time, wait for the heavy cone to catch up, and track only observed positions. Lost targets get a short remembered-lane scan; pressure, grenades and close targets trigger Sidekick/cover. Breach loads when preparing or empty, using the full player reload. Ordinary off-screen firing safeguards remain. Hidden lasers do not reveal players through walls. Lasers use two continuous instanced core/halo pieces; the armed rifle retains a wider brighter red beam, pale core and straight traveling amber highlights. The local placement guide draws after fog/decks without depth testing to avoid animated-muzzle/terrain gaps; physical flight and cover queries still end it at the first actual stop. Remote beams and all shots retain depth testing. Own guide has its own prewarmed batch so the lagging scope cannot hide it; remote beams and shots retain concealment. Camera/range edges use exact cached frustum geometry; fixed laser paths reuse cover queries and terrain samples until pose, aim distance, stance, aspect, ground or cover changes. Loading cannot leave the laser/wind batch hidden.
- Explicit `sightlineShot`, `sightlinePistol`, `sightlineBlast` death reactions preserve both dropped weapons and cleanup: bullet head wounds or ordinary explosion scatter.
- `CLAUDE_HANDOFF_OMEN.md` records all changed files, final verification and preview links for combining with Claude's map work. No commit, push, pull or merge without the owner.

The wider Breach beam makes them more cautious. Checks follow the actual elevated beam and cursor endpoint, with no reactions to empty/reloading guns, blocked beams or hidden shooters.
